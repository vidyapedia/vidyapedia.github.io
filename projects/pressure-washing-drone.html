01 / Goal

Bring façade cleaning hardware into the air.

The project explores an aerial platform for pressure-washing skyscraper façades. The systems challenge is not simply mounting a washer: flight, mass, power, fluid, vibration, communication, sensing, and safety all interact.

02 / Constraints

Every gram and amp matters.

The vehicle’s maximum takeoff mass is approximately 5 kg. Early integration showed how quickly the payload budget disappeared once the sprayer housing, filled tubing, battery, mounting hardware, and washer were counted together.

MTOWApproximately 5 kg
V1 system massApproximately 4.5 kg
V2 system massApproximately 4.1 kg
Washer current surgeApproximately 25 A
Integration consequencePower-switch selection, hose routing, water mass, and mechanical packaging had to be designed as one coupled problem.

03 / Architecture

Flight and payload, connected but separable.

A Pixhawk running ArduPilot provides the flight-control core. A Raspberry Pi 4 communicates over MAVLink and hosts camera-side compute. The Ryobi ONE+ HP 18 V washer is treated as a high-current payload with a dedicated trigger interface and suitably rated solid-state switching.

04 / V1 → V2

Move pressure generation onboard.

Version 1

Ground-based pump and heavier hose architecture created a demanding tether and mass distribution.

Finding

The fluid-delivery approach imposed penalties that could not be treated as a minor packaging issue.

Version 2

Onboard Ryobi washer with a siphon hose reduced total system mass and simplified the aerial interface.

05 / Bench test

Verify interfaces before flight.

Bench work validated USB MAVLink communication between the Raspberry Pi and Pixhawk, heartbeat reception, and attitude telemetry. Testing stayed intentionally off-aircraft while serial reliability, message streaming, trigger behavior, and power architecture were being established.

Systems habitDe-risk communication, power, and actuator interfaces on the bench before they become flight failures.
